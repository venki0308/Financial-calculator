How lumpsum growth is calculated

A one-time investment compounds on itself: each year's growth is earned on both the principal and all previous gains.

FV = P × (1 + r)t
P = investment   r = annual return ÷ 100   t = years

Frequently asked questions

How is lumpsum future value calculated?

FV = P × (1 + r)^t, where P is the one-time investment, r is the annual return (as a decimal) and t is the number of years. A ₹1 lakh investment at 12% for 10 years grows to ₹3,10,585.

How much does 1 lakh grow in 10 years?

It depends on the return: at 8% a ₹1 lakh lumpsum becomes ₹2,15,892, at 10% it becomes ₹2,59,374, at 12% it becomes ₹3,10,585 and at 15% it becomes ₹4,04,556 over 10 years.

Lumpsum or SIP — which is better?

A lumpsum puts your full amount to work immediately, which wins when markets rise steadily. A SIP spreads purchases across months, reducing timing risk. Many investors park a lumpsum in a liquid fund and transfer it gradually via STP.
