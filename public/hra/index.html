HRA Exemption Calculator — Old Regime 2026

Work out exactly how much of your House Rent Allowance is tax-free under Section 10(13A). Available only under the old tax regime — a key input when choosing your regime.

How HRA exemption is calculated

The exempt portion is the least of three amounts — anything above it is taxed as salary.

Exemption = min( HRA received,
  50% (metro) or 40% (non-metro) × (basic + DA),
  rent paid − 10% × (basic + DA) ), floor 0

Worked example (metro)

ComponentAmount / month
Basic + DA₹50,000
HRA received₹20,000
Rent paid₹18,000
Rule 1: HRA received₹20,000
Rule 2: 50% of basic+DA (metro)₹25,000
Rule 3: rent − 10% of basic+DA₹18,000 − ₹5,000 = ₹13,000
Exempt HRA (least of the three)₹13,000 (₹1,56,000 / year)

Metro vs non-metro — when it matters

The metro flag only changes Rule 2 (50% vs 40% of basic+DA), so it matters when rent is high. Example: basic+DA ₹50,000, HRA ₹30,000, rent ₹30,000 — in a metro the exemption is ₹25,000/month (least of ₹30,000, ₹25,000, ₹25,000), but non-metro it drops to ₹20,000/month (40% rule binds). In the ₹18,000-rent example above, Rule 3 binds in both cases, so metro status makes no difference.

Frequently asked questions

How is HRA exemption calculated?

The exempt HRA is the least of three amounts: (1) actual HRA received, (2) 50% of basic+DA in a metro city or 40% elsewhere, and (3) rent paid minus 10% of basic+DA. With basic+DA of ₹50,000, HRA of ₹20,000 and rent of ₹18,000 in a metro, the exemption is ₹13,000 per month (₹1,56,000 a year).

Can I claim HRA exemption under the new tax regime?

No. HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) is available only in the old tax regime. If you pay high rent, the HRA exemption can be a strong reason to compare both regimes before choosing.

Which cities count as metro for HRA?

From FY 2026-27 (Rule 279 of the Income-tax Rules 2026) eight cities count as metros for the 50% limit: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad. Until FY 2025-26 only the first four qualified; every other city uses 40%.

Do I need my landlord's PAN to claim HRA?

If your annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000, you must report the landlord's PAN to your employer to claim HRA through payroll. Keep rent receipts and, ideally, pay rent through bank transfer as proof.
