Worked example — property bought in FY 2010-11

Bought for ₹30 lakh (CII 167), sold in FY 2026-27 for ₹1.2 crore. Indexed cost = ₹30,00,000 × 384 ÷ 167 = ₹68,98,204. Indexed gain ₹51,01,796 × 20% = ₹10,20,359. The flat route is ₹90,00,000 × 12.5% = ₹11,25,000. The lower figure applies, so tax is ₹10,20,359 plus 4% cess = ₹10,61,174.

Frequently asked questions

What is the capital gains tax on shares and equity mutual funds in FY 2026-27?

Listed shares and equity mutual funds held for more than 12 months are long-term: gains above ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year are taxed at 12.5% (Section 112A, now Section 198 of the Income-tax Act 2025). Held 12 months or less, the gain is short-term and taxed at 20% (Section 111A / Section 196). A 4% cess applies on the tax.

Can I still use indexation on property sales?

Only for land or buildings bought before 23 July 2024, and only by resident individuals and HUFs. You pay the lower of 12.5% without indexation or 20% with indexation. Property bought on or after 23 July 2024 is taxed at a flat 12.5% after 24 months of holding.

How are debt mutual funds taxed?

Units bought on or after 1 April 2023 are specified mutual funds: the entire gain is taxed at your slab rate, whatever the holding period. Units bought before that date and held more than 24 months are taxed at 12.5% without indexation.

What is the CII for FY 2026-27?

384, notified by the CBDT on 15 July 2026. FY 2025-26 was 376 and FY 2024-25 was 363.
